## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** Mara, the consumer. A 38-year-old shift worker with two open claims and variable income, who opens the portal on her phone after a reminder and wants to settle her debt with a plan she can afford and keep.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** Settle both claims with a plan she can afford and keep, without making her situation worse.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** She reaches the income step and is asked for documents she can't easily find, with no explanation of why they're needed. She also can't tell whether a plan would be affordable or what happens if she misses a payment. Fearing a wrong answer could make things worse, she stops or calls.
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** Calling an agent, the main fallback named in the brief, where she repeats information she already entered.
Abandoning the journey and returning later, or not at all.
Possibly relying on letters or reminders to piece together what she owes. The brief doesn't confirm this, so it's worth checking in research.
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** People ready to pay us are giving up or missing payments within 60 days, and our own metric counts a plan as a success the day it's created, so failing plans and avoidable agent work stay hidden.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** For consumers like Mara who can't tell what's affordable or what happens if they miss a payment, A Fair Way Forward is a guided instalment journey in Riverty's existing portal with clear options and plain-language reasons. Unlike today's fragmented journey, it helps people agree to a plan they can keep, and gives agents like Daniel full context when a call is needed.

## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** She "isn't sure whether a plan would be affordable, why she's being asked about her income, what happens if she misses a payment, whether both claims can be handled together, or whether accepting an offer could make things worse."
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Started journeys ending in agreement, 46% (~4,514 of ~8,360 starters don't reach an agreement)

Journey starters contacting an agent within 7 days 31% (~2,590)

New agreements missing a payment within 60 days 19% (~730)
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Mara 38 a consumer, with the goal to Settle both claims with an affordable plan she can keep, without making her situation worse.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Mara wants to resolve her debt, but she can't tell which option is safe, so she gives up, calls an agent, or agrees to something she may not be able to keep.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** More consumers complete an affordable instalment plan on their own and keep it.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Instalments resolved with no inbound contact in the 30-day window and a plan still performing at 60 to 90 days.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Agreement rate among starters
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** The agreement rate is the SSR trap so we can't measure in days or weeks. Realistic to get valuable data from 60 days measurement, we should aim for 3-4 months before evaluating the outcome.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** I believe that giving Mara clear, plain-language options and consequences will raise agreement among starters from 46% to 50% and cut 60-day missed payments from 19% to 15%. I will protect complaint rate and agreement rate among starters, and decide go/no-go 60 days after enrolment closes (about 4 months).
