Slide 5 Strategy · Modules 1 and 3

The problem, the value, the bet

Problem statement hook

People ready to pay us are giving up or missing payments within 60 days, and our own metric counts a plan as a success the day it’s created, so failing plans and avoidable agent work stay hidden.

Value proposition

Affordable Plan Options replaces the document request at the income step with one question about what Mara can comfortably pay in a low month, then shows 2 to 3 plans sized to that amount, each with the monthly amount, number of payments, end date and total. It matters because 54% of started journeys don’t end in an agreement and 19% of new agreements miss a payment within 60 days. Clear options and plain reasons help more people agree to a plan they can keep, which a plan-created-day metric like SSR can’t show.

Data-backed hypothesis

Based on the 54% of started journeys that don’t end in an agreement, the 28% of income documents needing clarification and research themes 1 and 2, I believe that replacing document upload with one self-declared budget question and 2 to 3 sized plan options for Mara will lift self-service agreement among starters by at least 3 pp (target +4 pp from 46%). I will protect the 60-day miss rate (+3 pp margin vs. control), 7-day agent contact and complaints, and decide at enrolment close + 60 days after at least 4,349 starters per arm.

Slide 6 Research · Module 2

Mara hits a dead end, then calls

Competitive analysis / workaround summary

External tools

She calls customer support or sends an email, because it “felt easier”.

The process
  1. Opens the portal from the reminder on her phone, in a short gap between shifts.
  2. Looks for a plan option. She is routed to information pages, or the option is hidden by the debt-threshold defect.
  3. Tries to choose a monthly amount but can’t tell what is safe, and doesn’t want to experiment.
  4. Hits a dead end. A custom amount may trigger “Unable to process request” with no guidance, or her mobile session times out and wipes her data.
  5. Has no way back in. No continuation link is offered.
Core frustration

Hits a dead end. A custom amount may trigger “Unable to process request” with no guidance, or her mobile session times out and wipes her data.

Evidence

Who we solve for: Mara, 38, the consumer

Role

Mara, 38, the consumer (embodies “giving up”). A shift worker with variable income and two open claims, who handles her debt from her phone in the gaps of her day.

Goal

Find an affordable instalment plan she can trust, and set it up herself without having to explain her finances to an agent.

Friction: the moment of misery

She opens the portal from a reminder, reaches plan setup, and can’t tell what monthly amount is safe. If she tries a custom amount, she may hit “Unable to process request” with no guidance. If she pauses on her phone, the session can expire after 5 minutes and wipe her data. Either way, she is offered no link to resume. She then calls an agent, or forgets and never returns.

PRD: finalized Must-haves

  • One-question budget input, no documents. Ask “What can you comfortably pay each month in a low-income month?” and give a reason for asking it.
  • 2 to 3 rules-based plan options showing monthly amount, number of instalments, end date, and total to pay. Sized to her stated budget, with bounds and rules held as configuration, not hard-coded.
  • A plain-language “why this option” line on each option, for example “based on the amount you told us.”
  • Neutral presentation: no option pre-selected, none defaulting to the highest payment, and the lowest-burden option always visible.
  • “None of these fit” exit to an agent, passing the amount entered and the options viewed.
  • Confirmation step showing the plan terms, requiring an explicit confirm, with a way back. It reuses the F5 consequence panel from the Now lane.
  • Mobile-first layout in German, usable on a 375px-wide phone with no horizontal scroll.
What the PRD makes explicit

The prototype has no generative model. Every figure comes from a deterministic function that is checked against the balance before it renders, and every sentence is a static template with numbers inserted. Consequence text is a single compliance-approved string, never generated. If any check fails, the screen falls back to the agent route rather than showing unverified content.

Gap the prototype revealed

Some plans may show only one option. With the rules, amounts between €35 and €39 leave just one plan that stays within 24 months, so you’ll see one card instead of 2 to 3.

Slide 8 Validation · A/B experiment brief

The experiment plan

Hypothesis

I believe that Affordable Plan Options v1 (PRD M1 to M7 as one package): self-declared monthly budget, 2 to 3 sized plan options with “why this option”, consequence panel, confirm, agent exit for Mara, 38, consumer. Shift worker with variable income, opens the portal on her phone after a reminder. Goal: settle a claim with an affordable plan she can keep will result in self-service agreement among starters 46% to 50% (+4 pp), with the 60-day miss rate no worse than control, as measured by a +3 pp absolute (46% to 49%); target +4 pp change in self-service agreement rate among starters: share of journey starters who create a plan with no agent contact before plan creation. Minimum 4 weeks, run until at least 4,349 starters per arm (8,698 total). Miss-rate read 60 days after enrolment closes. We will protect the 60-day missed-payment rate among new agreements (baseline 19%) throughout the test.

Control (A)

Current German portal instalment journey for the pilot segment, unchanged. After opening the portal from a reminder or collection letter, the consumer starts an instalment request and is asked for income information, with income documents uploaded at the income step. No reason is given for why income is requested, and no plan options sized to the consumer’s situation are shown before commitment. Documents that are unclear or unreadable go to manual review or clarification by an agent. Phone and email routes to an agent remain available at all times.

Variant (B)

The income step changes from document upload to one question (“What can you comfortably pay each month in a low-income month?”, with a “why we ask” line), and the answer produces 2 to 3 plan options sized to that amount, each showing monthly amount, number of payments, end date and total. No option is pre-selected. Everything after the option is chosen is identical to control.

  • Eligibility rules, and the minimum and maximum plan terms the control could already grant
  • Confirmation step, consequence wording and payment processing
  • Agent availability, staffing and routing, including phone and email
  • Any other backlog release (for example “Why We Ask” labels or the consequence panel) in the pilot segment during the test

Slide 9 Launch · Module 6

GTM strategy and success dashboard

Goal: Conversion Tier: S, Minimal Audience: pilot segment, Germany
Goal

Conversion. The feature’s job is to help more consumers who start an instalment request finish a plan on their own and keep it. The north-star SSR can rise while plans fail, so the goal is a sustainable agreement, measured by agreement rate (baseline 46%, target +4 pp) with the 60-day miss rate (baseline 19%) protected. Awareness is not needed: consumers are already sent to the portal by existing outreach.

Audience

Primary: consumers who start an instalment request in the German portal in the pilot segment, like Mara (38, shift worker, variable income, on her phone after a reminder). Secondary: collection agents like Daniel, who take escalated cases and need the handoff to work. Internal stakeholders who must be ready: compliance, data protection, and client account managers.

Launch tier: S, Minimal

S for consumers: the pilot covers one segment in Germany, outreach and notifications stay unchanged, and no consumer-facing announcement is made, so the A/B result stays clean, and will run for a minimum of 4 weeks. Silence carries little risk here because the feature appears at a point the consumer already reaches. The real launch risk is internal (agents who don’t know the new flow, compliance gaps on German copy), so enablement effort is high even though reach is low.

Channels
Owned · Reminder and collection letter Owned · In-journey content Owned · Agent channel (internal)
  • Existing reminder and collection letter, with digital reminder, leading to the portal. Unchanged in content and timing. This is the only way consumers arrive, and holding it constant is what keeps the test valid.
  • In-journey content in the portal: the “why we ask” line, option cards and consequence information are the launch. Mara learns about the feature by using it at the income step.
  • Agent channel (internal): briefing, a one-page guide and escalation notes, so agents like Daniel recognise cases that came through the new journey and know what the consumer saw.
Enablement and assets
  • Agents (Daniel): short briefing on the new journey, a one-page cheat sheet on what consumers see, how “None of these fit” cases arrive, and how to log overrides.
  • Compliance and data protection: sign-off pack covering German copy, the self-declared budget, retention period, and plans without documents.
  • Clients and account managers: one-pager explaining the pilot, the control group, and the stop triggers.
  • Assets to build: German UI copy in a single strings file, consequence wording (compliance-approved), “why we ask” text, agent guide, stop-trigger dashboard.
  • Not needed: consumer announcement, email campaign, press.

Bad signal to watch for

Agreement rises while the 60-day miss rate rises with it, or first-payment failure runs 5 pp or more above control (placeholder). That means more plans created and fewer kept: the SSR trap. Action: pause exposure and review plan sizing, with compliance authorising and engineering switching off the flag within one business day.

Most likely post-launch decision

Iterate, most likely, because the 60-day read may be inconclusive at the available sample. Scale (25%, then 50%) if agreement is up by at least 3 pp, the miss-rate upper bound stays below +3 pp, and contact and complaints hold. Kill if the full-sample agreement interval cannot reach +3 pp, or the miss rate is 3 pp or more above control with the interval excluding zero.

Slide 10 Story · Individual insights

What was hard, what clicked

Friction points and the aha moment

The hardest part was resisting the easy win. Agreement rate and SSR are simple to move, but SSR counts a plan as resolved the day it’s created, so it can rise while plans fail. Writing the experiment brief made it worse before it got better: my first variant bundled four changes, and my guardrail was the primary metric’s own baseline.

The aha moment was seeing that the sample size wasn’t the problem. The pilot segment’s volume was, because the whole illustrative universe yields only about 8,360 starters a month, so a 4-week test could never reach significance.

Key takeaways / what I would do next
1

A metric you can’t hold to account (SSR) needs a sustainability partner (plan performing at 60 to 90 days).

2

A guardrail must be a different metric the change could damage, with a margin and a control to compare against.

3

Test one change at a time, and size the duration from real segment volume, not the calendar.
