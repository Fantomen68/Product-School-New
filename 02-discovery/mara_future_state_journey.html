Mara's future state journey

Persona: Mara, 38, a shift worker with variable income and two open claims, handling her debt from her phone in the gaps of her day.

Strategy: Reclaim operational simplicity by stripping away legacy noise, so the tool is the fastest for everyday work.

Value proposition: Fair Way Forward helps consumers like Mara choose an affordable instalment plan they can keep, with clear options and plain-language reasons, and gives agents like Daniel full context when they do need to step in.

  1. 1

    Arrive and orient

    User action

    Taps reminder → lands on plan setup, not information pages.

    Internal state

    Hurried but hopeful; expects a quick, self-managed task.

    Pain point addressed

    Information-page loops and hidden options.

    UXR-02, BUG-1017
  2. 2

    Choose with confidence

    User action

    Compares affordable ranges with plain reasons → picks an amount without guessing.

    Internal state

    Cautious; needs proof the amount is safe and fair.

    Pain point addressed

    No amount guidance; "Unable to process request" dead end.

    UXR-03, BUG-1044
  3. 3

    Pause without penalty

    User action

    Leaves mid-setup → progress saves and a resume link arrives.

    Internal state

    Interrupted by shift work; fears starting over.

    Pain point addressed

    Five-minute timeout wipes data; no continuation link.

    BUG-1031, BUG-1088
  4. 4

    Commit, or hand off with context

    User action

    Confirms plan → submission succeeds first time, no restart.

    Internal state

    Relieved and in control; trusts she can keep the plan.

    Pain point addressed

    Post-confirmation submit failures; agents re-checking everything.

    BUG-1062, OPS-01
Agent handoff (Daniel) Mara requests help → Daniel sees her entries and reasons, nothing repeated.

Competitive advantages over the manual workaround

  1. Speed and accessSelf-serves any hour on her phone → no waiting for agent hours.
  2. Privacy and dignityChooses a plan with plain reasons → no explaining finances to a stranger.
  3. VisibilityEvery start, pause, and finish is tracked → abandonment becomes measurable, not invisible.

Assumptions to validate

  • Stage 2: The affordable range needs affordability rules and a compliance review. The research doesn't show how ranges would be calculated.
  • Both claims together: This comes from the case study, not our research, so it is left out of the stages.
  • Advantage 3: Depends on new tracking of abandoned attempts. BUG-1088 suggests we lack this today, but that is an inference, not a documented finding.
